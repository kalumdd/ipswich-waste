Hoarder clearance: what to expect

Hoarding is recognised as a mental health condition, and clearing a hoarded home is very different from a normal clearance. The person living there may find it distressing to see belongings removed, and rushing the process can make things worse. Our approach is patient, respectful and led by the person and the people supporting them.

We work with individuals, families, social workers, housing officers and mental health teams across Ipswich. Some clearances happen in one go, for example when a property must be made safe quickly. Others are done in stages over weeks, with the person deciding what stays and what goes.

Hoarded properties can contain hazards: blocked exits, structural strain from weight, mould, pests, rotting food and sharps. Our team wears protective equipment, works carefully, and can arrange pest control and deep cleaning once the clearance is done.

Exits and walkways cleared first, with hazards dealt with safely.

Discretion

We can use unmarked vehicles and keep the work low-key for neighbours.

Follow-on cleaning

Deep cleaning, sanitising and pest control arranged once the property is clear.

Supporting someone through a clearance

Involve the person

Wherever possible, the person who lives in the home should be part of the decisions. A clearance carried out without their involvement often leads to the problem returning.

Work with professionals

If a social worker, housing officer or mental health team is involved, we coordinate with them so the clearance fits with any wider support plan.

Searching for important items

Hoarded homes often contain important documents, cash, medication and personal items. We sort carefully and set these aside.

Health and safety

Where there is a risk to health, such as mould, pests or biohazards, we use protective equipment and may arrange specialist cleaning before or after the clearance.
