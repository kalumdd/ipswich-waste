Probate house clearance: what to expect

Clearing the home of someone who has died is one of the hardest jobs a family faces. It often comes at a time when there is already a lot to deal with: the funeral, the will, the estate and the house itself. We aim to take the physical work off your hands, carefully and without rushing you.

We carry out probate and bereavement clearances across Ipswich for families, executors, solicitors and estate agents. If family members want to visit the property first and take items they would like to keep, we work around that. If you would rather not be there at all, we can manage the whole clearance and keep you updated.

Throughout the clearance, our team looks out for anything that might matter: photographs, letters, documents, jewellery, medals and keepsakes. These are boxed separately and returned to you, along with anything that may be needed for the estate, such as bank statements, share certificates or insurance papers.

Probate clearances and the estate

When to clear the property

Executors can arrange a clearance before probate is granted, but it is sensible to make sure the will is understood and any specific gifts have been identified first. If in doubt, check with the solicitor handling the estate.

Keeping the estate informed

We provide a written quote and invoice addressed to the executor or the estate, and can send copies to the solicitor. You receive a waste transfer note for the clearance.

Council and housing association properties

Tenancies often need to be ended within a set period after a death, and the property returned empty. We can work to those deadlines and liaise with the landlord if needed.

Selling or donating contents

Items with value can be sold to offset the cost of the clearance. We can tell you what is likely to have resale value and what is better donated.

Security of an empty house

Empty properties can attract attention. We can clear quickly once you are ready, so the house does not sit full and unattended for long.

Frequently asked questions

Can you clear a house before probate is granted?

Yes, in many cases. Check with the executor or solicitor that specific gifts in the will have been identified before the clearance.

What happens to personal papers and photographs?

We box all documents, photographs and personal items separately and return them to you.

Can family visit before the clearance?

Of course. We encourage family to take anything they would like to keep before we begin.

Do you work with solicitors and executors?

Yes. Quotes and invoices can be addressed to the estate, with copies sent to the solicitor.

Can you meet a council or housing association deadline?

Yes. Tell us the date the property has to be returned and we will plan around it.

Do you clean the property afterwards?

Yes. Every clearance ends with a sweep and vacuum, with deeper cleaning available.
